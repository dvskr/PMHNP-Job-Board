## Step-by-Step Licensure Guide

Delaware is a member of the NLC and has enacted the APRN Compact, which is not yet operational. Single-state endorsement takes **4-6 weeks**.

### Step 1: Complete MSN or DNP with PMHNP Focus

Graduate from an accredited advanced practice program. Coursework must include advanced health assessment, diagnosis/management in your specialty, advanced pathophysiology, and advanced pharmacology.

### Step 2: Pass the ANCC PMHNP-BC Exam

Maintain active national certification. The ANCC sets the exam fee, and discounts it for members of the ANA and AANP:

| Membership Status | Exam Fee |
|:---|:---|
| Non-member | $395 |
| ANA member | $295 |
| AANP member | $340 |

Verify the current fee with the ANCC before you register.

Learn more about the [PMHNP-BC credential](/blog/what-does-pmhnp-bc-mean)

### Step 3: Apply for Your DE RN License

Delaware is an **NLC state**. If your primary state of residence is another NLC state, your multistate RN license is valid here.

### Step 4: Apply for Your DE APRN License

Submit your APRN application to the Delaware Board of Nursing. The **APRN Compact** is not yet operational, so no multistate APRN privilege exists: you need a Delaware APRN license even if your home state has enacted the compact.

### Step 5: Obtain Prescriptive Authority

Prescriptive authority is **included in your licensure** (legend drugs granted unless you decline). 

### Step 6: Register for DEA and State CSR

| Credential | Details |
|:---|:---|
| **Delaware Controlled Substance Registration (CSR)** | Required for Schedule II-V |
| **Federal DEA registration** | The DEA charges $888 for a three-year registration. Verify the current amount with the DEA. |

### Step 7: Apply for an NPI Number

Obtain your National Provider Identifier (NPI) via the [CMS NPPES system](https://nppes.cms.hhs.gov/) (Free).

## DE Practice Authority Details

**Current status:** Delaware is a **Full Practice Authority** state, with no collaborative agreement and no transition period.

APRNs may independently perform advanced assessment, diagnosing, prescribing, and ordering. NPs are **legally recognized as primary care providers** and may be designated as such by insurers.

**Day-to-day impact:**
You can independently sign:
- ✅ Death certificates
- ✅ Advanced health care directives
- ✅ DMOST forms (with specific training)

## Prescriptive Authority in DE

**What can PMHNPs prescribe?**
Full authority to prescribe, procure, administer, store, dispense, and furnish OTC medications, legend drugs, and controlled substances. Can also plan nonpharmacological interventions.

**Schedule II-V rules:**
Full independent prescribing of **Schedule II through V** controlled substances.

**Practice gap rules:**

| Gap in Practice | Requirement |
|:---|:---|
| **2+ years** | Additional pharmacology CE + advanced practice refresher required |

## Telehealth Rules in DE

**Can PMHNPs do telehealth?**
Yes. Delaware reimburses for live video telehealth, and the Mid-Atlantic Telehealth Resource Center (MATRC) supports providers in the region.

**Out-of-state requirements:**
Delaware has enacted **both** the NLC and the APRN Compact, but the APRN Compact is not yet operational: it takes effect only once seven states enact it, and NCSBN said in April 2026 that it is redesigning the compact. Out-of-state PMHNPs must hold a **Delaware APRN license** to treat patients in Delaware via telehealth.

**Controlled substance teleprescribing:**
Federal DEA and HHS flexibilities that allow prescribing controlled substances via telemedicine without a prior in-person evaluation have been extended through **December 31, 2026**.

## Loan Repayment & Incentive Programs

Practicing in Delaware's shortage areas unlocks loan forgiveness:

| Program | Award Amount | Service Requirement |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Delaware State Loan Repayment Program (SLRP)** | State-funded through the Delaware Health Care Commission, which sets the annual pool and the individual award | Typically 2 years in underserved area |
| **NHSC SUD Workforce LRP** | Up to **$75,000** | 3 years full-time at an NHSC-approved SUD facility |
| **NHSC Rural Community LRP** | Up to **$100,000** | 3 years full-time at a rural NHSC-approved facility |

The NHSC maxima are set by the federal National Health Service Corps; the SLRP pool is set by the Delaware Health Care Commission. Both change between funding cycles, so verify the current amount with the awarding body before you apply.

## Tips for PMHNPs Moving to DE

1. **Watch the APRN Compact:** Delaware was among the first to enact the APRN Compact, but the compact is not yet operational and NCSBN is redesigning it. Until it takes effect, you need a Delaware APRN license to practice in DE.

2. **Track Your Practice Hours:** Biennial renewal requires attestation to either **1,500 hours** over 5 years, **600 hours** over 2 years, or graduation within the past 2 years.

3. **Audit Your CE Mandates:** 30 CE hours must include **3 in substance abuse** and **1 in abuse/trafficking/domestic violence**. Prescribers need **2 additional hours** in controlled substance prescribing.

4. **Get Your State CSR:** Don't forget the Delaware Controlled Substance Registration. It is separate from your DEA number.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is Delaware a Full Practice Authority state for PMHNPs?**
A: Yes, with no transition period or collaborative agreement.

**Q: Can PMHNPs in Delaware prescribe Schedule II controlled substances?**
A: Yes, with full independence. Requires DEA registration and state Controlled Substance Registration (CSR).

**Q: Is Delaware part of the Nurse Licensure Compact (NLC)?**
A: Yes, for RN and LPN licenses. Delaware has also enacted the APRN Compact, but that compact is not yet operational, so PMHNPs still need a Delaware APRN license.

**Q: What are the CE requirements to renew my DE PMHNP license?**
A: Complete 30 CE hours every two years (3 substance abuse + 1 abuse/trafficking + 2 controlled substance for prescribers) plus practice hour attestation.
