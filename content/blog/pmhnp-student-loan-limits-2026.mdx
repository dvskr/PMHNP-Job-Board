**Quick answer:** Grad PLUS loans ended for new borrowers on July 1, 2026. What replaces them for PMHNP students depends on a court case. Today, the Department of Education is treating MSN and DNP programs as "professional" degrees, which allows up to **$50,000 a year and $200,000 in total**. That treatment exists only because a federal court stayed part of the Department's rule. If the stay ends, the limit drops to **$20,500 a year and $100,000 in total**. Budget against the lower number.

---

This is the largest change to how nurse practitioner education is financed in a generation, and it arrived in pieces: a statute, a regulation, a lawsuit, and an administrative workaround. Here is each piece in order, with what it means if you are choosing or already attending a PMHNP program.

## What the Law Changed on July 1, 2026

Public Law 119-21, signed in July 2025, rewrote graduate borrowing.

- **Grad PLUS ended.** For any period of instruction beginning on or after July 1, 2026, a graduate or professional student is not eligible for a Federal Direct PLUS Loan.
- **New annual and total caps apply to Direct Unsubsidized Loans.** The amount depends on whether you are a graduate student or a professional student.
- **A lifetime ceiling applies to everyone.** Total federal student borrowing is capped at $257,500, not counting PLUS loans a borrower takes out as a parent for a dependent child.

| Federal limit | Graduate student | Professional student |
|---|---|---|
| Annual Direct Unsubsidized Loan | $20,500 | $50,000 |
| Total for graduate or professional study | $100,000 | $200,000 |
| Lifetime, all federal student loans | $257,500 | $257,500 |

Before this law, Grad PLUS let a student borrow up to the full cost of attendance. For a PMHNP program that costs more than the new caps allow, the difference now has to come from savings, employer support, scholarships, or private loans.

## Why the Limit Depends on a Court Case

The statute sets two tiers but leaves the definition of "professional student" to the Department of Education. The Department's final rule, published May 1, 2026 and effective July 1, 2026, concluded that the MSN does not qualify as a professional degree. That placed nurse practitioner students in the lower tier.

Nursing organizations and a group of states sued. On June 24, 2026, the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia preliminarily stayed that part of the rule. Five days later, Federal Student Aid told schools that, for the duration of the stay, it is treating a list of programs as professional for loan limits. The list includes:

- Registered Nursing (MSN), CIP code 51.3801
- Nursing Practice (DNP), CIP code 51.3818

A July 10 update extended that treatment to any program in the same four-digit CIP family that awards the same credential.

The Department was plain about how temporary this is. It said the designations "may change as litigation in the case proceeds." It also told schools they "may wish to consider" limiting loans to the graduate caps for these programs, to protect students from a mid-program change. That means your school may hold you to the lower limit even while the stay is in place.

Final briefing in the case is scheduled to finish in early December 2026, according to a litigation summary from a higher education law firm. A decision could come any time after that, and either side could appeal.

## Who Keeps the Old Rules

The law protects students who were already in a program. If, as of June 30, 2026, you were enrolled and had received a federal Direct Loan for that program, the old limits continue to apply for your "expected time to credential." The statute defines that as the shorter of three academic years or the time remaining in your program.

Two details matter:

- The protection is tied to that program of study, so check with your aid office before you change programs or schools.
- Enrolling part-time reduces your annual limit in proportion under the new rules, so confirm with your aid office how your enrollment status is counted.

## How to Budget if You Start in 2026 or 2027

1. **Plan against $20,500 a year.** If you end up eligible for more, you can decline it. The reverse is much harder.
2. **Ask the financial aid office two questions.** Which CIP code does the school report for your program, and has the school set its own program-level limit?
3. **Price the whole program, not the first year.** Total cost against a $100,000 cap is the number that decides whether you need other funding.
4. **Treat private loans with care.** They do not qualify for federal income-driven repayment or Public Service Loan Forgiveness.
5. **Ask employers early.** Tuition assistance and sign-on support for psychiatric roles are negotiable, and some health systems fund the degree for staff nurses who commit to stay.
6. **Compare program length.** A shorter program at a lower total cost can beat a longer one even at the same annual borrowing limit.

## Repayment After the Change

New borrowers choose between a standard plan and the new Repayment Assistance Plan. Under the statute, the payment is a percentage of adjusted gross income that rises with income, from 1 percent for incomes just over $10,000 to 10 percent for incomes over $100,000. It is reduced by $50 a month for each dependent and has a $10 minimum. Remaining balances are forgiven after 360 qualifying payments. Payments on the plan count toward Public Service Loan Forgiveness.

That last point matters for PMHNPs, because a large share of psychiatric jobs are with nonprofit health systems, community mental health centers, and government agencies.

## Loan Repayment Programs Worth Knowing

Federal service programs can retire a meaningful part of a balance in exchange for work in shortage areas. The amounts below are the most recent published awards, as summarized by the Rural Health Information Hub. HRSA sets them each cycle, and the 2026 application windows have closed, so verify the current figures on HRSA's site before you plan around them.

| Program | Most recent award | Service commitment |
|---|---|---|
| NHSC Loan Repayment Program | Up to $75,000 for primary care, up to $50,000 for other eligible disciplines | 2 years |
| NHSC Substance Use Disorder Workforce | Up to $75,000 | 3 years |
| NHSC Rural Community | Up to $100,000 | 3 years |
| Nurse Corps Loan Repayment | 60 percent of the balance, plus 25 percent for an optional third year | 2 years |

Your site must be approved before you apply, so ask about program eligibility when you interview. Community mental health centers, federally qualified health centers, and rural health systems are the most common participating employers, and you can search current openings on the [PMHNP job board](/jobs).

## What This Means for Choosing a Program

Cost now matters more than it did when Grad PLUS could cover any tuition. A program that requires you to borrow well past the federal cap leaves you financing the rest privately, at rates and terms that offer none of the federal protections. Weigh that against expected pay.
