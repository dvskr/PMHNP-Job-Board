**Quick answer:** The Bureau of Labor Statistics reports a median of **$63.61 an hour** for nurse practitioners of all specialties, as of May 2025. BLS does not publish a PMHNP figure. For psychiatric roles, our [salary guide](/salary-guide) publishes the median of what live PMHNP postings advertise, with an hourly equivalent. Whatever number you start from, an hourly rate only means something once you know how many hours are paid, whether the role is W-2 or 1099, and what benefits come with it.

---

Hourly quotes are common in psychiatric work: telehealth contracts, PRN shifts, locum assignments, and per-visit arrangements are all priced by time or by encounter. The trouble is that two offers with the same hourly number can differ by tens of thousands of dollars a year. This guide shows how to put any offer on the same footing.

## What Official Data Says About NP Hourly Pay

These are BLS figures for nurse practitioners as a whole occupation, covering every specialty.

| BLS measure, May 2025 | Hourly | Annual |
|---|---|---|
| Median | $63.61 | $132,300 |
| Mean | $66.01 | $137,300 |

The spread is wide. BLS puts the 10th percentile annual wage at $101,340 and the 90th percentile at $174,420. It annualizes hourly wages on a 2,080-hour basis, which is a full-time, year-round schedule.

Two cautions apply. This is not a psychiatric figure, because BLS does not break the occupation down by specialty. It is also survey data on wages paid to employees, so it does not capture what independent contractors bill.

## Converting Hourly to Annual: The Schedule Decides

The same hourly rate produces very different yearly totals depending on paid hours. The table below is arithmetic, not market data. The second column assumes 40 paid hours a week for 52 weeks. The third assumes 35 paid hours a week for 48 weeks, which is closer to many outpatient and contract schedules once unpaid time off is counted.

| Hourly rate | 40 hours, 52 weeks | 35 hours, 48 weeks |
|---|---|---|
| $60 | $124,800 | $100,800 |
| $70 | $145,600 | $117,600 |
| $80 | $166,400 | $134,400 |
| $90 | $187,200 | $151,200 |
| $100 | $208,000 | $168,000 |
| $120 | $249,600 | $201,600 |
| $140 | $291,200 | $235,200 |

## W-2 Hourly and 1099 Hourly Are Not the Same Dollar

A W-2 hourly rate and a 1099 hourly rate are different products.

**Taxes.** A contractor pays self-employment tax. The IRS sets that rate at 15.3 percent of net earnings, made up of 12.4 percent for Social Security and 2.9 percent for Medicare. An employee pays half of that, and the employer pays the other half.

**Benefits.** A W-2 role usually comes with employer contributions to health insurance and retirement, paid time off, malpractice coverage, and often licensure and continuing education support. A contractor buys all of those.

**Unpaid time.** Contractors are frequently paid only for completed visits. Documentation, messages, refill requests, and prior authorizations may be unpaid.

**Deductions.** Contractors can deduct legitimate business expenses, which offsets some of the difference.

The honest way to compare is take-home pay plus the value of benefits. Our [1099 vs W-2 calculator](/tools/1099-vs-w2-calculator) does that side by side and shows the break-even hourly rate a contract needs to match a salaried package. The [1099 tax guide](/blog/pmhnp-1099-tax-guide-2026) covers quarterly payments and deductions.

## Turning Per-Visit Pay Into an Hourly Figure

Per-visit contracts need one more step. The method:

1. Multiply the per-visit rate by the number of paid visits you complete in a clinical hour.
2. Work out what share of your working week is unpaid: documentation, messages, no-shows that are not compensated, meetings.
3. Reduce the clinical hourly figure by that share. The result is your effective hourly rate.

Here is an illustration with invented numbers. Substitute the rates in your own contract. Suppose a contract pays $50 for a follow-up and you complete two an hour. That is $100 per scheduled clinical hour. If a quarter of the hours you work are unpaid, your effective rate is $75 an hour. If the contract pays nothing for no-shows and one visit in ten does not show, it falls further.

Ask these before you accept a per-visit rate:

- What is paid for a no-show or a late cancellation?
- How long is each visit type, and who decides?
- Is there a guaranteed minimum while your panel fills?
- Is time for documentation and messages paid?

## Locum, PRN, and Telehealth Quotes

Short-term and flexible roles are almost always quoted hourly, and the numbers often look higher than salaried pay. They are higher because they leave things out: benefits, paid leave, and continuity. Locum assignments may add travel and housing, which changes the total again.

None of that makes them a bad choice. It means you should compare a year of each. You can see what current postings advertise on the [1099](/jobs/1099), [per diem](/jobs/per-diem), and [locum tenens](/jobs/locum-tenens) job pages.

## Five Questions That Make Any Offer Comparable

1. How many hours a week, and how many weeks a year, will I be paid for?
2. Am I an employee or a contractor?
3. What is the dollar value of the benefits, and which ones do I have to buy myself?
4. Which parts of my working time are unpaid?
5. What does the rate become after the first year, and what triggers a raise?

Answer those five and you can convert any quote into a yearly total you can trust. From there, negotiation is a matter of knowing the market. Our [salary negotiation guide](/blog/pmhnp-salary-negotiation) covers how to make the ask.
