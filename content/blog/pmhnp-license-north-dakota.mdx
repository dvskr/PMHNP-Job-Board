> **Key Takeaway:** North Dakota is a **Full Practice Authority** state and the **first state to enact the APRN Compact** (March 2021). You get full Schedule II-V prescribing (a separate application, a $75 fee set by the ND Board of Nursing, and **30 pharmacology hours**), **12 CE hrs** biennially (plus **15 pharmacology** and **400 practice hrs** every 4 years), recognition as a **PCP**, and authority to sign **DNR orders, death certificates, and advance directives**. HRSA's shortage-area data puts ND at only **34.87%** of mental health needs met, so demand is high. For pay, see [current advertised pay for PMHNPs in North Dakota](/salary-guide/north-dakota).

North Dakota is an exceptionally progressive state for PMHNPs. Notably, North Dakota was the **very first state** to implement the APRN Compact, making it a national leader in nursing mobility.

Despite this highly favorable environment, HRSA, which designates Mental Health Health Professional Shortage Areas, reports that ND meets only **34.87%** of its mental healthcare needs and requires **18 additional practitioners** to eliminate its HPSAs. Here is your guide to obtaining your PMHNP license in the Peace Garden State for 2026.

## Step-by-Step Licensure Guide

ND has **both** NLC and APRN Compact. Single-state endorsement takes **2-4 weeks**, one of the fastest turnarounds nationally.

### Step 1: Complete MSN or DNP with PMHNP Focus

Graduate from an accredited advanced practice program. Must complete **30 contact hours** of pharmacotherapy education related to your psychiatric scope.

### Step 2: Pass the ANCC PMHNP-BC Exam

Maintain active national certification. The ANCC sets the exam fee and discounts it for members of the ANA and the AANP. Verify the current amount with the ANCC before you apply.

| Membership Status | ANCC Exam Fee |
|:---|:---|
| Non-member | $395 |
| ANA member | $295 |
| AANP member | $340 |

Learn more about the [PMHNP-BC credential](/blog/what-does-pmhnp-bc-mean)

### Step 3: Apply for Your ND RN License

North Dakota is an **NLC state**. If your primary state of residence is another NLC state, your multistate RN license is valid here.

### Step 4: Apply for Your ND APRN License

Submit your APRN application to the North Dakota Board of Nursing. If your home state participates in the **APRN Compact**, your multistate APRN privilege covers ND.

### Step 5: Apply for Prescriptive Authority

Submit a **separate application** with evidence of 30 pharmacotherapy hours. The North Dakota Board of Nursing sets the fee, currently **$75**. Verify the current amount with the board before you apply.

### Step 6: Register with the DEA

Apply for your federal DEA registration using your North Dakota practice address.

- **Fee:** The DEA sets the registration fee, currently around $888 for a three-year term. Verify the current amount with the DEA before you apply

### Step 7: Apply for an NPI Number

Obtain your National Provider Identifier (NPI) via the [CMS NPPES system](https://nppes.cms.hhs.gov/) (Free).

## ND Practice Authority Details

**Current status:** North Dakota is a **Full Practice Authority** state, with no transition period and no collaborative agreement.

APRNs can independently assess clients, synthesize data, formulate diagnoses, and prescribe therapeutic regimens. State statute formally recognizes NPs as **primary care providers** under the medical assistance program with the same rights as primary care physicians.

**Day-to-day impact:**
You can independently sign:
- ✅ Advanced directive forms
- ✅ DNR orders
- ✅ Death certificates

---

## Prescriptive Authority in ND

**What can PMHNPs prescribe?**
APRNs can order medical devices, nutrition, diagnostic services, and prescribe drugs and controlled substances independently.

**Schedule II-V rules:**
Full prescribing of **Schedule II through V** controlled substances, including pharmaceutical samples.

**Requirements:**
- Separate application plus the Board of Nursing's $75 fee
- 30 contact hours of pharmacotherapy education
- Federal DEA registration

## Telehealth Rules in ND

**Can PMHNPs do telehealth?**
Yes. Telehealth is essential across ND's vast rural regions, and the state has Medicaid reimbursement parity.

**Out-of-state requirements (unmatched mobility):**
As the **first APRN Compact state**, PMHNPs with a multistate APRN license can practice telehealth in ND **without a separate license**.

**Controlled substance teleprescribing:**
Federal DEA and HHS flexibilities have been extended through **December 31, 2027**.

Both change between funding cycles, so verify the current amount with the administering body before you apply.

| Program | Award Amount | Service Requirement |

## Tips for PMHNPs Moving to ND

1. **Leverage the APRN Compact:** ND was the first state to enact it. If your home state also participates, you can practice here on your multistate privilege with no separate license needed.

2. **Track Your Practice Hours:** Renewal requires **400 practice hours** over the preceding 4 years.

3. **Audit Your CE Requirements:** Biennial renewal needs 12 CE hours, plus **15 pharmacology hours** for prescribers.

4. **Apply Separately for Prescribing:** Prescriptive authority requires its own application, the Board of Nursing's $75 fee, and 30 pharmacotherapy hours.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is North Dakota a Full Practice Authority state for PMHNPs?**
A: Yes, with no transition period. NPs are recognized as primary care providers with the same rights as physicians under the medical assistance program.

**Q: Can PMHNPs in North Dakota prescribe Schedule II controlled substances?**
A: Yes, with full independence. It requires a separate prescriptive authority application (the Board of Nursing's fee is currently $75) and DEA registration.

**Q: Is North Dakota part of the NLC and APRN Compact?**
A: Yes to both. ND was the **first state** to enact the APRN Compact (March 2021).

**Q: What are the CE requirements to renew my ND PMHNP license?**
A: Complete 12 CE hours every two years plus 400 practice hours over 4 years. Prescribers need an additional 15 pharmacology hours.
