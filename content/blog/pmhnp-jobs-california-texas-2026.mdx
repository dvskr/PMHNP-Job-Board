California and Texas together account for a large share of PMHNP job openings nationwide. Whether you're a new grad deciding where to start your career, an experienced psychiatric NP considering relocation, or a telehealth provider choosing which licenses to prioritize, these two state markets deserve a thorough comparison.

## California PMHNP Market Overview

### Job Market Snapshot

- **Total PMHNP openings:** The largest pool in the country. [Browse current California openings](/jobs/state/california)
- **Advertised pay:** Among the highest headline numbers nationally. [See current advertised pay in California](/salary-guide/california)
- **Practice authority:** Restricted, transitioning under AB 890
- **State income tax:** Statutory marginal rates of 9.3% to 13.3% at PMHNP income levels, the highest in the nation
- **Top metro areas:** Los Angeles, San Francisco/Bay Area, San Diego, Sacramento, Inland Empire
- **Mental health shortage:** HRSA designates 46 of California's 58 counties as Mental Health Professional Shortage Areas (MHPSAs)

### Practice Authority: AB 890 Changes Everything

California has historically been a restricted practice state, requiring physician supervision for PMHNPs. However, **Assembly Bill 890** is changing the landscape:

- **Phase 1 (current):** NPs with 3+ years of experience in specified settings (health systems, group practices, clinics with MD oversight) can apply for independent practice authority through the new NP Practice Committee
- **Phase 2 (expected 2026-2027):** Broader independent practice for NPs meeting experience requirements
- **Impact:** This transition makes California increasingly attractive for PMHNPs, with FPA-equivalent practice expected within 2-3 years
- **Current limitation:** The NP Practice Committee and transitional process are still evolving; implementation has been slower than the bill originally envisioned

See our [Full Practice Authority Guide](/resources/fpa-guide) for the complete state breakdown.

### California Pay by Setting

Settings do not pay the same, and the spread between them is wide enough to matter more than the state you pick. The table below ranks California settings against each other rather than quoting figures we cannot source. For actual numbers, [see current advertised pay for PMHNPs in California](/salary-guide/california), which is a median of live listings.

| Setting | Relative Pay | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| Outpatient/Community Health | Lower band | FQHCs offer NHSC loan repayment |
| Hospital/Inpatient | Upper-middle band | Kaiser, Sutter, UC systems |
| Private Practice (employed) | Upper band | Productivity bonuses common |
| Private Practice (owner) | Highest, and the most variable | Highest earning potential, and the most risk |
| Telehealth/Remote | Wide range, middle to upper | Many CA-based telehealth companies |
| VA/Federal | Lower band on base pay | Federal benefits (pension, EDRP, FEHB) close much of the gap |
| Correctional (CDCR) | Upper-middle band | State prison system; excellent benefits |

### Top Employers in California

1. **Kaiser Permanente:** Largest integrated health system, excellent benefits, union-represented NPs, and among the strongest pay in the state
2. **Sutter Health:** Major Northern California system with growing psychiatric programs
3. **UCLA Health / UC System:** Academic medical centers with research opportunities across UC campuses
4. **LA County DMH:** Government positions with loan forgiveness eligibility and retirement benefits
5. **CDCR (CA Dept of Corrections):** State prison system; competitive salary, CalPERS retirement, high demand
6. **Telehealth companies:** Talkiatry, Cerebral, Lyra Health (many headquartered in the Bay Area)
7. **Community Health Centers:** Hundreds of FQHCs across CA, NHSC-eligible

### California Licensing Requirements

- **Board of Registered Nursing (BRN)** processes APRN applications
- **Standardized procedures** or supervisor agreement required (until AB 890 fully implements)
- **Furnishing number** required separately for prescriptive authority
- **Processing time:** 8-16 weeks, one of the slower states, so plan ahead
- **DEA registration** required for controlled substances
- **Continuing education:** 30 hours every 2 years
- **Background check:** Fingerprinting through LiveScan

## Texas PMHNP Market Overview

### Job Market Snapshot

- **Total PMHNP openings:** One of the three deepest markets in the country. [Browse current Texas openings](/jobs/state/texas)
- **Advertised pay:** Lower headline numbers than California, higher take-home for many. [See current advertised pay in Texas](/salary-guide/texas)
- **Practice authority:** Restricted (physician supervision required)
- **State income tax:** **0%. Texas levies no state income tax**
- **Top metro areas:** Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth, San Antonio, Austin, El Paso
- **Mental health shortage:** HRSA designates 207 of Texas's 254 counties as MHPSAs, among the most severe shortfalls in the nation

### Practice Authority: Still Restricted

Texas remains a restricted practice state with no imminent legislative changes:

- **Prescriptive authority agreement** with a supervising physician required
- **Physician delegation** must be within a 75-mile radius (with exceptions for telehealth and underserved areas)
- **Controlled substances:** Schedule III-V with physician agreement; Schedule II requires additional physician authorization
- **Supervision protocol:** Must be filed with the Texas Board of Nursing
- **Impact on daily practice:** Most large employers provide the collaborating physician as part of employment, which makes the restriction more of an administrative burden than a clinical one
- **Finding a collaborator independently:** If you arrange your own collaboration agreement for a private or small group practice, expect a recurring monthly fee. Rates vary widely by specialty and by how much chart review the physician takes on, so get quotes before you build a budget

### Texas Pay by Setting

As in California, the setting matters more than most candidates expect. The table below ranks Texas settings against each other. For actual numbers, [see current advertised pay for PMHNPs in Texas](/salary-guide/texas), which is a median of live listings.

| Setting | Relative Pay | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| Outpatient/Community Health | Lower band | FQHCs and CMHCs |
| Hospital/Inpatient | Middle band | HCA, Baylor, THR systems |
| Private Practice (employed) | Upper-middle band | Productivity models common |
| Private Practice (owner) | Highest, and the most variable | Need physician collaboration |
| Telehealth/Remote | Wide range, lower to middle | Growing rapidly in TX |
| Correctional/Federal | Middle band | TDCJ, BOP, and VA |
| Rural/Underserved | Upper band | Sign-on bonuses are common here |

### Top Employers in Texas

1. **HCA Healthcare:** Largest for-profit hospital chain in the US, major Texas presence across 80+ hospitals
2. **Baylor Scott & White:** Largest non-profit health system in Texas, strong behavioral health programs
3. **Texas Health Resources:** Major DFW metro system with growing psychiatric services
4. **UT Health System:** Academic systems across Houston, San Antonio and Dallas, with research and clinical opportunities
5. **Texas DSHS/HHSC:** State government positions with benefits, retirement, and loan forgiveness
6. **MHMR Centers:** Local mental health authorities across all 254 counties; highest psychiatric demand

### Texas Licensing Requirements

- **Texas Board of Nursing (BON)** for APRN licensure
- **Prescriptive authority application** filed separately through the BON
- **Processing time:** 4-8 weeks (significantly faster than California)
- **Supervisory agreement** must be on file with the BON before prescribing
- **Continuing education:** 20 hours every 2 years (pharmacology component required)
- **Background check:** Fingerprinting through Identogo
- **PDMP registration:** Texas Prescription Access in Clinical Texas (PACTx) required

### Financial Deep Dive: Where Does Your Dollar Go Further?

The tax and housing gap is large enough that a lower gross offer in Texas can beat a higher one in California. The table below is a worked example, not a claim about what either job pays: it assumes a hypothetical $185,000 California offer against a hypothetical $155,000 Texas offer, housing at $3,500 a month in California and $1,600 a month in Texas, and single-filer tax treatment. Swap in your own numbers and the arithmetic still runs the same way.

| Category | Example: California ($185K) | Example: Texas ($155K) |
|----------|-------------------|---------------|
| Federal income tax | -$35,000 | -$28,000 |
| State income tax | -$16,500 (about 9%) | **$0** |
| **Net after taxes** | **$133,500** | **$127,000** |
| Assumed housing cost | -$42,000/year | -$19,200/year |
| **After housing** | **$91,500** | **$107,800** |

In this example Texas comes out roughly **$16,300 a year ahead** on discretionary income despite the $30,000 lower gross. The gap widens further once you factor in car insurance, childcare, and general consumer prices. Run the same comparison against the [California](/salary-guide/california) and [Texas](/salary-guide/texas) advertised-pay medians and your own housing costs before you decide.

### Which State Is Better for Your Career Stage?

**Choose California if:**
- You want to be near the most prestigious academic health systems (UCLA, UCSF, Stanford-affiliated)
- You value the cultural and recreational lifestyle of coastal California
- You plan to build a private practice and want FPA (coming under AB 890)
- You work in telehealth and want to serve the largest single-state patient population in the country
- You have family or roots in California

**Choose Texas if:**
- You want to maximize take-home pay and build wealth faster
- You're buying a home and want real estate affordability
- You want fast licensing and quick entry into practice
- You're drawn to rural practice, where HRSA designates 207 of 254 counties as shortage areas
- You prefer lower overall cost of living and no state income tax

**Choose both (multi-state license):**
- If you work in telehealth, holding licenses in both CA and TX gives you access to the two largest patient populations in the country

| Factor | California | Texas | Winner |

| **Pay after state tax** | Reduced by a top statutory rate of 13.3% | Untouched, no state income tax | Texas |
| **Pay after cost of living** | Eroded by the highest housing costs in the country | Stretches considerably further | Texas |
| **Practice authority** | Restricted (CA requires physician supervision) | Restricted (TX requires collab agreement) | Tie |

### The California Strategy

If California is where you want to live, maximize your earnings by: targeting hospital or health system positions in the Central Valley or Inland Empire, where advertised pay holds up well against LA and SF while housing costs far less; stacking your W-2 salary with 1099 telehealth moonlighting serving patients in other states; and pursuing loan repayment through California's MHSA-funded program for public mental health service. Verify the current award and service term with the administering state agency before you count on it.

### The Texas Strategy

Texas offers the best financial value for most PMHNPs. Maximize by: settling in a mid-size metro such as San Antonio, Austin or Fort Worth, where the cost of living is lower than Houston or Dallas and pay is comparable; building toward private practice, where the recurring cost of a collaboration agreement is real but the no-tax advantage usually makes up for it; and leveraging the state's large VA footprint for federal benefits and EDRP eligibility.

### Emerging Markets Within Each State

**California's hidden opportunities:** While Los Angeles and San Francisco are saturated, the Inland Empire (Riverside, San Bernardino), Central Valley (Fresno, Bakersfield), and Sacramento metro offer pay close to coastal markets at a far lower cost of living. Community health centers in these areas also qualify for NHSC loan repayment.

**Texas growth corridors:** Austin's tech-driven population growth is creating massive mental health demand, with new behavioral health clinics opening quarterly. The Dallas-Fort Worth Metroplex has 7+ major health systems actively recruiting PMHNPs. San Antonio's military presence creates VA and DoD contract opportunities, while the Rio Grande Valley has acute shortages with significant loan repayment incentives.

### Relocation Incentives Comparison

| Incentive | California | Texas |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------|
| Sign-on bonus | Commonly offered | Commonly offered, and typically larger |
| Relocation assistance | Sometimes offered | More often offered, and typically larger |
| Student loan repayment | California's MHSA-funded program | Texas State Loan Repayment Program |
| Housing assistance | Rare | Moderate (rural areas) |
| CME allowance | Standard in most offers | Standard in most offers, often more generous |

Both loan repayment programs set their own maximum awards and change them between funding cycles, so verify the current amount with the administering state agency before you factor it into an offer.

Texas employers generally offer more aggressive incentive packages than California because the competition for PMHNPs is intensifying in a market that has historically relied on physician psychiatrists.

## Florida, New York, and Other Major Markets

Beyond California and Texas, here is a quick look at three other major PMHNP markets:

**Florida:** No state income tax, restricted practice authority. Miami and Tampa dominate job volume. The retirement population drives geriatric psych demand. Cost of living varies sharply between Miami and Jacksonville, which changes the real value of the same offer considerably. [See current advertised pay in Florida](/salary-guide/florida).

**New York:** A top statutory rate of 10.9%, and Full Practice Authority after 3,600 supervised hours. NYC carries the highest headline numbers but its cost of living makes it one of the worst value markets. Upstate NY (Rochester, Buffalo, Syracuse) pairs competitive offers with mid-tier costs, which makes it the state's hidden gem. [See current advertised pay in New York](/salary-guide/new-york).

**Georgia:** A 5.49% state tax rate and restricted practice authority. Atlanta is a rapidly growing market with healthcare system expansions at Emory, WellStar, and Piedmont driving demand. Outside Atlanta, rural Georgia offers NHSC-eligible positions with significant loan repayment. [See current advertised pay in Georgia](/salary-guide/georgia).
